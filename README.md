# TrailPlanner — GPX route planner

[AI-enabled Vercel app](https://gpxdesign.vercel.app/) · [GitHub Pages static app](https://stanleycheng.github.io/gpx_design/). A responsive single HTML frontend with embedded CSS and JavaScript. **Routes are provisional planning candidates, not certified safe navigation tracks.**

## Workflow

1. Add mandatory places using decimal coordinates, TXT/CSV, GPX, image recognition, or map pins.
2. Review each place and its order. Select regional guidance, hike date, distance, transport approach, waypoint tolerance and road-connector limits. In Hong Kong, **Official trail stream crossings** can explicitly admit a mapped ford on matching AFCD trail geometry; it is off by default.
3. Find up to three distinct routes. Every route must include every place within the accepted tolerance and follow real connected OSM walking ways. An open route must start and end near passenger stops linked to mapped transit services; a loop instead starts and finishes at waypoint 1 and ignores transport.
4. Choose a route, inspect the full map and evidence, independently check access and transport, then save GPX. After reversing, review arrival and departure services for the new direction; reversal is disabled when mapped foot direction or boarding rules prohibit it. PNG remains a clearly labelled planning preview.

The bottom panel starts minimized on every page load and shows only a handle; tap or click it to open the controls. “Put pins on the map” is selected by default, and pin-adding mode is already active when the map loads. Tap or click the map immediately to place waypoints; open the controls to finish adding or switch input methods. It has five stages: Adding Pins, the chosen input tool, Requirements, Routes and Export. Selecting a method opens its matching input tab; accepted points advance to Requirements. Tabs preserve entered data, and later stages become available when their required data exists. Planning status sits beside the logo, with the circular **Find** control directly below the header on the right and horizontally centred with the route circles. During a search, Find cycles slowly through red, green and blue while three dots bounce below its label; it remains steady light green while idle, and reduced-motion mode shows steady dots. The **Pins** toggle above **Export** hides numbered pins and S/F markers without changing the routes or GPX. Route labels remain visible. Starting pin editing shows markers again. Instructions and information cards are hidden inside the panel; feedback appears above it, and selected-route evidence opens separately through Details. Export safeguards remain active.

**Loop** in Requirements is off by default. When enabled, every candidate starts at waypoint 1 and returns to that exact same snapped map coordinate. Public transport is neither required nor downloaded for a loop. The full mapped return counts toward distance and road limits. At least two pins are required, every pin remains mandatory, and Route 1 is the shortest qualifying mapped candidate found. Reordered alternatives, when eligible, remain anchored at waypoint 1. A loop may retrace paths, including an out-and-back; it does not promise a circuit with no repeated sections. If the directed walking graph cannot return to waypoint 1, the app explains the failing connection and never adds an artificial closing line.

The engine returns fewer options or an actionable failure instead of inventing routes or silently relaxing limits. It searches actual walking metres in pin order first, then evaluates eligible alternatives; the final candidates are sorted by actual mapped distance so Route 1 is the shortest qualifying candidate found. Equal-distance candidates prefer the entered pin order. Alternatives prefer hiking relations, clear tagged paths and nearby official trail corridors, penalise roads and retracing, and may reorder up to 8 pins only for a shorter walk or better mapped-trail score. Strict order remains selectable. These preferences are not current-condition or safety assessments. It is a bounded heuristic over downloaded map data, not a globally optimal itinerary, current-condition safety assessment or timetable planner. The default urban runner/hiker setting permits any amount of eligible mapped walking geometry up to the overall 80 km road bound, which lets city loops follow legal pedestrian streets instead of making long trail-seeking detours. The 3 km trail-focused limit remains selectable. Neither setting admits motorways, trunks, private or prohibited access, unsuitable cycleways, impassable terrain, or roads that fail the existing pedestrian eligibility rules. Transport for open routes is sought within a 1 km walking approach at each end first. If an end lacks a reachable service-linked stop, only that end’s map search expands progressively to 4 km, 10 km and at most 20 km; the other end keeps its nearby-stop preference. The first successful bounded search stops further downloads. Both approaches, plus the disclosed stop gaps, count toward the hike limit. The 20 km bound is checked on walking distance, not just a straight-line circle. A 30 m default waypoint tolerance is explicit, reviewable and adjustable. For each waypoint, snapping considers up to 24 deterministic eligible segment candidates within that exact tolerance, reserving candidates from distinct path clusters. A layered directed search chooses the connected pin-order combination with the smallest total snap offset; mapped walking distance and stable segment identity break ties. Loop snapping additionally requires all chosen candidates to share a directed strongly connected component, so the final leg can return over real geometry. One-way foot rules remain active. Visually crossing ways stay disconnected unless OSM supplies a shared node, and no synthetic connector fills any waypoint or transport-stop gap.

## Regional coverage and evidence

- **Hong Kong:** worldwide OSM graph plus a live AFCD hiking-trail geometry query. Approximate corridor matches use 12 m tolerance, not proof of ownership, management or current access. If AFCD is unavailable, the app says so.
- **Taiwan, Japan, South Korea:** the same OSM walking and transport-service graph, plus relevant official authority links. Government trail geometry, current closures and timetables are **not integrated** for these regions yet.
- **Elsewhere:** experimental, subject to OSM coverage. Limited to local searches between 75°S and 75°N, a core waypoint area at most 250 km², not crossing the date line; at most two 20 km endpoint circles can be added for transport. A response remains bounded to 64 MiB and 500,000 OSM elements. Loop queries omit transport objects, which reduces dense-city downloads without shrinking the walking graph.

Up to 50 mandatory places across map pins, coordinate/GPX imports, and recognized waypoints; reordering alternatives are considered for up to 8. With more pins, alternatives retain the entered order. The maximum hike choice is 80 km. The default urban runner/hiker road allowance permits a long city walk while still requiring eligible mapped pedestrian geometry; stricter trail-focused limits remain available. It does not admit motorways, trunks, private roads or any excluded access. All selected limits are enforced. A bus-stop symbol without a mapped service does not qualify for an open route. Stops must be within 80 m of a route-network node, with that unrouted gap explicitly shown. The hike date is for manual timetable checks, not an automatic schedule filter. No ascent, duration, safety score or elevation is invented. Known restrictions, conditional access, demanding `sac_scale` tags, unclear trail-visibility tags, impassable surfaces, via ferrata and unsuitable roads are excluded; untagged conditions remain unknown. Public OSM pedestrian corridors, including indoor station passages, are eligible and still obey mapped access, barriers, levels and foot direction. Fords are excluded by default. The optional Hong Kong setting admits only ford segments matching AFCD geometry and never overrides `foot=no`, private or conditional access, locks, barriers, or one-way rules. Route review and GPX descriptions disclose every admitted ford.

## Image input

[Kimi recognition](AI_SETUP.md) is the main route-map workflow: it identifies a suggested area and visible marked places only through the deployed private Vercel function and a user-initiated Identify action. After review, the selected places feed the same three-option route planner. Kimi never supplies the routing graph. Each selected place still needs one valid coordinate; unsupported coordinates remain blank. No endpoint secret or provider key is shipped to the browser.

[Manual image conversion](IMAGE_CONVERSION.md) still works locally: calibrate three known locations, select a continuous route colour, trace and review. Image traces and imported tracks are unverified source references, not traversability evidence. GPX import preserves segments and numeric elevations; if no waypoints exist, only segment endpoints become mandatory places. Add any other must-visit locations explicitly.

The planning map opens ready to add pins. Numbered waypoints use 24 × 30 px teardrops with 44 × 44 px touch targets; the tip marks the coordinate. On desktop, click to place a pin, drag to pan and use the wheel or trackpad to zoom; right-click a pin to remove it. On iPhone, tap to place a pin, drag the map with one finger, pinch with two, and tap a pin for Delete. Drag a pin to move it. Open the panel to finish adding pins or review their order and route limits. In Requirements, click or tap the coordinates below any waypoint to copy the displayed latitude and longitude to the system clipboard. In Routes, swipe left/right over the option cards with two fingers on a Mac trackpad (or one finger on iPhone). Ordinary vertical scrolling still moves the panel. Focus the card row to browse with Left/Right, Home and End; browsing does not change selected or visible routes. Tabs support arrow-key, Home and End navigation; the panel scrolls independently and respects the phone’s bottom safe area.

## Run, test and publish

Node 22.12 or newer, with no third-party runtime packages required:

```sh
node scripts/build-inline.mjs
npm test
npm run build
node --env-file=.env server/recognition.mjs
```

Open `http://127.0.0.1:8787/`. Copy `.env.example` to a private `.env` if one does not already exist; never overwrite a populated key file. The server serves an explicit public-file allowlist and will not serve `.env`, source files or tests. No Kimi calls are possible without a configured key and the user selecting Identify.

The authored routing engine and UI fragments in `lib/` are embedded into `index.html` by the build script. The deployed frontend stays one HTML file plus icons. Leaflet 1.9.4, Exifr 7.1.3, fonts and map tiles load online. No offline navigation is claimed. Vercel publishes the allowlisted assets, private recognition function and server-side route planner. The GitHub Pages and local-file frontends use that Vercel route planner first, with direct public-provider access retained as an emergency fallback. See [AI_SETUP.md](AI_SETUP.md).

## Privacy and operations

Drafts and files stay in browser memory except recognition images/clues sent when the user selects Identify. Recognition serializes a resized canvas, stripping camera metadata; the server forwards to Kimi and does not write images to disk. Remote hosting requires a separate private app access token; see setup notes. There is no daily recognition quota, so monitor Kimi usage and billing directly.

Routing sends waypoint coordinates and the chosen route limits to the TrailPlanner Vercel function. That function validates and bounds the request, sends the derived bounding box and, for an open route if required, endpoint search circles to the selected Overpass provider and (Hong Kong only) the AFCD service, computes the three candidates, and returns only the compact route results. A loop request omits passenger stops, stop areas and transit relations from its Overpass query. Providers see the area and Vercel server address; Vercel processes the waypoints. Uploaded files and map images are not sent by route planning. OSM tiles still reveal the viewed area to the tile provider, and PNG reuses displayed tiles. The browser retries an interrupted route-server connection up to three times before reporting a retryable failure; it does not replace a dense server plan with a memory-heavy direct browser download. Ten-minute server memory caching, sequential provider fallback, bounded downloads and explicit timeouts reduce public-service load. High-traffic deployment still needs a dedicated or commercial routing data service.

A missing transport endpoint triggers bounded 4 km, 10 km and 20 km downloads with separate cache keys, stopping at the first successful radius. Off-path pins, disconnected mandatory legs, invalid input and provider failures do not trigger a transport expansion. Failure messages identify the affected pin, disconnected leg, missing arrival/departure service or exceeded length/road constraint; route failures remain visible above the panel until another action replaces them. Route functions have a 240-second ceiling, with shorter individual-provider and overall timeouts. [Vercel duration configuration](https://vercel.com/docs/functions/configuring-functions/duration) and [Overpass around queries](https://dev.overpass-api.de/overpass-doc/en/full_data/polygon.html) document these mechanisms.

Map queries request hiking relations belonging to the downloaded paths, plus transport services belonging to nearby passenger stops or their stop areas. They do not download every transit route crossing the bounding box. Automatic mode checks available cached map data first, then tries FOSSGIS, Private.coffee and VK Maps in sequence. A smaller query changes download scope without relaxing walking access rules, waypoint inclusion, transport approach limits or the distinct-route requirement. One qualifying route remains one route; the app does not invent alternatives.

Optional Google Analytics is consent-based and production-only; coordinates, files, photos, typed clues and access codes are never analytics parameters. See [ANALYTICS.md](ANALYTICS.md). `.env`, private inputs, exports, runtime ledgers and common generated files are ignored by Git. The header's noninteractive “Early preview” badge has been removed.
