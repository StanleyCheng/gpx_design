A little more considered

Official trails come first

Government-managed routes are the priority. A mapped path alone is not proof of official status or safe access.

Get there. Get home.

Both ends need usable public transport, with service dates and last departures checked—not just a nearby stop.

No made-up connections

Waypoints stay separate until a real trail connection can be verified. Fewer qualifying routes is always acceptable.

Files stay local unless you choose AI recognition.

On-device conversion

Locate three known places, pick the route colour, then mark the start, any via points, and the end. We follow connected coloured pixels; we never fill a gap. The result is an unverified image trace, not a verified hiking route.

Choose a tool below, then tap the image.

Tap or click to place a point. For precision, zoom and scroll the image. Keyboard: focus the image, use arrow keys (Shift for larger steps), then Enter. Escape cancels the current tool.

A/B/C = known locations · 1/2/3… = route order · cyan = extracted pixels

1. Locate the map

Use three well-spaced, non-collinear landmarks or grid intersections, ideally around the route. Enter their independently verified WGS84 latitude, longitude. Photo GPS alone cannot calibrate a map.

0 of 3 image locations marked. Wrong coordinates or projection will produce the wrong GPX.

2. Extract the visible line

Mark at least two points in hiking order. Add via points on every intended branch. For a loop, add via points and finish at the start.

Lower values isolate the route more strictly. Increase only if the line varies in colour. Black, white, dashed, obscured or disconnected routes need a clearer source image.

Your image stays on this device. No AI service is called.

3. Review before exporting

Inspect the cyan line at every junction: same-colour branches can produce the wrong path. Then compare its position on the independent basemap below. No government status, closures, terrain or transit has been verified.

The GPX remains an unverified image reference and is not suitable for navigation. Saving is enabled only after you preview the complete trace on the basemap.

    Built with care

    Know what’s behind
    your route.

    Clear limits are part of the product.
    Here’s what the planner can—and cannot—verify.

    How do I create and save a route?

    Add your must-visit places, check the numbered pins and visit order, choose your limits, and select “Find up to 3 routes”. Each candidate follows connected OpenStreetMap walking geometry through every listed place within the tolerance you chose. Choose a route, inspect its transport and trail evidence, then save GPX. After reversing, review the arrival and departure services again. Input-only GPX export remains separate and does not create a walking route.

    What is verified, and what still needs checking?

    The routing engine checks graph connectivity, every mandatory waypoint, known access and difficulty restrictions, foot direction, mapped transit-service membership, distance and road limits. Search is limited to 250 km², 16 places in entered order, or 8 with order optimisation. Hiking date is a reminder for your checks; it does not filter timetables. A nearby transport stop can still be inaccessible or out of service.

    Hong Kong’s AFCD trail geometry is checked when its public service is available. Taiwan, Japan and South Korea use the same worldwide OSM graph with regional official guidance links; government management is not automatically established there. No live closures, permits, weather, timetables or terrain inspection are integrated. Missing information stays unknown. No ascent, walking-time estimate or safety score is invented. The gpx-trail-fix principle is retained: generated track geometry comes only from actual mapped ways.

    How are map photos handled?

    Images stay local unless you select Kimi recognition through a private server. Kimi can suggest the map area and marked places, but each coordinate needs independent review. A server connection is required on GitHub Pages. “Paste a route map → GPX” accepts a flat screenshot, three known coordinate references, and a selected continuous route colour. It extracts adjacent matching pixels in the order you mark, then requires a visual review. The converter does not automatically identify an unknown region, correct angled photos, bridge gaps, or verify trails. A wrong projection, calibration or same-colour branch can still produce a wrong result. GPS in a photo may describe the camera location and is never used to locate the depicted map.

    Where does my data go?

    This app does not store your draft between visits. Files stay local except when you explicitly send a map image and optional clue to your configured recognition server and Kimi. Camera metadata is stripped from that upload. Route search sends a bounding box to the selected Overpass provider and, for Hong Kong, the AFCD public map service. These services see the requested area and your IP address. Never put private details in image clues. Google Analytics loads only if you allow optional analytics. It measures page views and fixed app-action names, plus standard browser/device and approximate location data. We do not send coordinates, filenames, photos, GPX contents, typed text, query strings, or referrer URLs to Analytics. Automatic enhanced measurement and advertising features are disabled. You can withdraw consent using “Privacy settings” below. Browser privacy signals and blockers may prevent tracking.

    Your analytics choice is stored locally; analytics cookies, if accepted, are limited to this project. GitHub hosts the page; Google Fonts and the pinned Leaflet and Exifr libraries load from external providers. OpenStreetMap receives requests for the map tiles you view, which reveal the approximate area and your IP address. PNG export uses tiles already displayed; it does not download an offline map.
